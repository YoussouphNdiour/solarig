Residential solar installation: hybrid inverter, lithium battery and protection box

How a home solar installation works, how to estimate consumption and size panels and battery, with the real example of a 5.8 kWp installation in Diamaguène (Thiès).

Produce, store, use: the principle of a hybrid installation

A hybrid residential solar installation performs three operations: produce electricity with panels, store the surplus in a battery, then use that energy in the evening, at night or during a grid outage. The sun powers the house first; whatever is not consumed charges the battery.

This is the layout of the installation we carried out in Diamaguène, Thiès. Its logic applies to any home, as long as each element is sized to the household's real consumption.

The four components: panels, hybrid inverter, battery, protection box

  • Solar panels: they produce direct current. Their total power is expressed in kWp.
  • Hybrid inverter: it converts direct current to alternating current for the house, manages battery charging and switches to the battery during an outage. Its power in kW sets what you can draw at the same time (air conditioner, pump, iron).
  • Lithium battery: it stores energy. Its capacity is expressed in kWh; the energy actually usable depends on the depth of discharge, often 80 to 90% for lithium.
  • Protection box: it groups the safety devices between the panels, the inverter and the home's wiring.

Our solar installation service covers the whole chain, from study to installation.

Step 1: estimate your consumption

For each appliance, multiply its power by its running time and add them up. Indicative example for a household:

  • Refrigerator: about 1.5 kWh per day.
  • 6 LED bulbs of 8 W for 5 h: 0.24 kWh.
  • 3 fans of 60 W for 8 h: 1.44 kWh.
  • 100 W television for 5 h: 0.5 kWh.
  • An 800 W air conditioner for 6 h: 4.8 kWh.
  • Miscellaneous appliances: 1 kWh.

Total: 1.5 + 0.24 + 1.44 + 0.5 + 4.8 + 1 ≈ 9.5 kWh per day. Your electricity bills or an on-site measurement give a more reliable figure than these typical values.

Step 2: size the battery, then the solar array

Estimate the share of consumption in the evening and at night. If it is 60% of the 9.5 kWh, the battery must supply 9.5 × 0.6 ≈ 5.7 kWh. With a depth of discharge of 80 to 90%, the nominal capacity to plan is 5.7 ÷ 0.9 to 5.7 ÷ 0.8, i.e. 6.3 to 7.1 kWh.

For the solar array, we reuse the logic of our tools: 5.5 equivalent sun hours per day and losses of 0.8, i.e. 4.4 kWh produced per kWp per day. Adding about 10% battery losses (0.57 kWh): (9.5 + 0.57) ÷ 4.4 ≈ 2.3 kWp, i.e. 4 panels of 580 W (2.32 kWp). These are orders of magnitude: the visit specifies orientation, shading and roof.

The real example: Diamaguène, 10 panels, 8 kW hybrid, 15 kWh lithium

In Diamaguène (Thiès), SOLARIG installed 10 panels of 580 W (5.8 kWp), an 8 kW hybrid inverter, a 15 kWh lithium battery and a protection box. The installation produces, stores and uses solar energy.

With the same assumptions, 5.8 kWp produce on the order of 5.8 × 5.5 × 0.8 ≈ 25 kWh per day. The 15 kWh battery shifts part of that production to the evening and night, while the 8 kW inverter leaves headroom for powerful appliances. See the details in the project page.

The protection box: your home's safety

The protection box is not an accessory. Depending on the installation, it generally groups circuit breakers and an isolator on the direct-current and alternating-current sides, lightning and surge protection (surge arrester) and the earthing connections. It lets the installation be shut down safely for work and protects the equipment in case of a fault.

Tidy wiring, suitable protections and a clean, labelled technical room make the difference over time: this is what we check at every commissioning.

How to start your project

Gather your latest electricity bills, the list of your appliances and, if possible, photos of the roof or land. We then size the array, inverter and battery and give you a detailed quote. Request your quote: an on-site visit refines the figures before any order.

Frequently asked questions

How many panels does a house need?

It depends on consumption. For example, a household using 9.5 kWh per day with a battery needs about 2.3 kWp, i.e. 4 panels of 580 W, while the Diamaguène installation has 10 panels of 580 W (5.8 kWp). A study based on your bills or on site gives the right number.

What lithium battery capacity should I choose?

The battery must cover what you use in the evening and at night. Divide that energy by the depth of discharge, often 80 to 90% for lithium: 5.7 kWh to store requires about 6.3 to 7.1 kWh of nominal capacity. The Diamaguène installation uses a 15 kWh battery.

Do I need a hybrid inverter?

If you want to store energy in a battery and have electricity during an outage, yes: the hybrid inverter handles current conversion, battery charging and switchover during a cut.

What happens during a grid outage?

With a hybrid inverter and a battery, the house keeps running on the panels and the battery. Duration depends on the stored energy and the power drawn: an air conditioner drains the battery faster than LED lighting.
